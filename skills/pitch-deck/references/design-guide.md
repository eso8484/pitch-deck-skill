# Pitch deck design guide

Distilled from Sequoia's pitch structure, Pitch.com startup templates, and visual-hierarchy principles. These are the rules that separate a professional deck from a generated-looking one.

## Story (Sequoia order, adapt as needed)
1. **Cover**: company name, logo, a one-line promise, hero product shot.
2. **Problem**: whose pain, how big (one sourced hero number), why today's options fail.
3. **Solution**: what the product is, in one sentence, plus 3–4 pillars.
4. **Product** (2–4 slides): real screenshots, one capability per slide, benefit-led headline.
5. **Why now / Market**: sourced numbers and a bottom-up calculation shown as arithmetic.
6. **Business model**: who pays, real prices from the product.
7. **Traction / where we are**: honest. "Live, pre-revenue" beats vague claims.
8. **Team**: only what the founder confirms.
9. **Closing**: the vision in one sentence, plus contact.

Leave out what the audience didn't ask for: credits applications, cloud costs, partner names, internal tooling.

## Visual rules
- **One message per slide.** The headline states the takeaway as a sentence; highlight 2–4 key words with `<mark>`.
- **Hierarchy through size and weight.** A big display headline (46–84px, weight 800), a small uppercase eyebrow label in the accent color, then body text at 15–20px in a muted color. Never more than three type sizes on one slide.
- **Rhythm.** Alternate dark statement slides (cover, solution, big claims, closing) with light content slides. Never place two busy slides back to back.
- **Whitespace is the design.** Generous margins (80px sides). If a slide feels empty, make the elements bigger rather than adding more of them.
- **Real product imagery.** Real screenshots in phone or browser frames always beat icons and stock imagery. Crop screenshots to the part that matters; empty app pages look broken.
- **Brand.** Take colors, logo and tagline from the real product. Use one typeface (the template embeds Plus Jakarta Sans), one accent color and one dark base.
- **Numbers.** Show a few at a large size with a short label underneath. Put the source in small text on the slide.
- **Tracking.** Big bold type needs only slight negative letter-spacing (−0.015 to −0.025em). Tighter than that and words start to collide.
- **Consistency.** The same footer, margins, eyebrow style and frame shadows on every slide.

## Common failure modes (seen and fixed)
- Generic card grids on every slide make a deck look like a template. Vary the layouts: hero number, split text and screenshot, a phone row, stats with a calculation card.
- `[TBD]` boxes all over the slides look unfinished. Restructure the slide so it doesn't need the unknown fact (e.g. a "Founded by …" line instead of a team grid with no roles), and list the gaps for the founder separately.
- Overstuffed slides: more than about 40 words of body text means split the slide or cut words.
- Screenshots of empty states, error banners, "expired" badges, or real personal data. Pick better screens or crop them out.
